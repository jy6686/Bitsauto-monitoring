No, we do not send rates through the service plan. The service plan is read, never written.

The plan is confirmed, and it reconciles the screenshot. Plan 38 is named "1global" and points at tariff 68. The Sippy accounts page shows the name while the API shows the id, so both are the same object. Plan 38 is also the only plan pointing at tariff 68, so nothing else shares it.

Where the rate actually goes. The write is scoped to the tariff, not the account and not the plan. The upload token is requested with i_tariff as its only scope, and the workbook carries prefixes and prices. No part of that path reads or modifies a service plan, and this morning's push proved it: the rate went to tariff 68 and the account saw it without plan 38 being touched.

read  →  account 1069 → plan 38 → tariff 68     (to find the destination)
write →  tariff 68                              (the rate itself)
So the plan's only job here is to answer "which tariff does this account bill on". That is why the integrity check matters. It compares the tariff the platform recorded against the tariff Sippy reaches through the plan, and refuses if they disagree, because writing to the resolved one would price a tariff the customer does not own, and writing to the stored one would price a tariff they do not use.

And that is exactly what the two test companies expose. Both sit on plan 1, named "Junaid", which points at tariff 2. So Sippy bills them through a plan named after a person, on a tariff shared with whatever else uses plan 1, while the platform believes they have tariffs 62 and 63. The plan assignment, not the rate, is what is wrong for those two.

That distinction also answers the design question underneath yours: giving a customer their own pricing means giving them their own plan pointing at their own tariff, which 1global has and those two do not.
