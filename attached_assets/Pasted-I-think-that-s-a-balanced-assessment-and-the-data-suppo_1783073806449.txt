I think that's a balanced assessment, and the data supports a more nuanced conclusion than "everything is fixed."

There are three separate health indicators here:

Component	Status	Confidence
AMI governance timer (7s cut)	✅ Healthy	High
Catastrophic vendor leak (350–765s)	✅ Appears resolved	Medium–High
End-to-end billing verification	⚠️ Incomplete	Low (limited by CDR coverage)

The first two are encouraging, but the third is the one I'd focus on before declaring success.

What the data tells us

The strongest evidence is this:

gov_seconds = 7 consistently.
The extreme overruns (350–765 s) have disappeared after June 30.
Daily vendor duration averages are now around 12 seconds, not hundreds of seconds.

That strongly suggests the system is behaving much closer to the intended design.

The remaining ~5-second average difference is not, by itself, alarming. It can arise from:

SIP answer timing versus bridge timing,
network propagation,
BYE processing,
vendor CDR rounding,
or when the vendor starts billing relative to your governance timer.

Without packet captures or SIP logs, it's difficult to attribute those few seconds to any single cause.

The real weakness is visibility

The biggest operational concern is this:

Only ~600 of ~9,800 governed calls have a matched vendor CDR.

That means you're making conclusions from roughly 6% of the governed traffic.

This doesn't mean governance is failing—it means your verification pipeline isn't giving you enough evidence.

I would prioritize this next

Instead of spending more time tuning governance, I'd improve CDR reconciliation reliability.

My priority order would be:

Increase CDR match coverage (highest priority)
Investigate why getSippyCDRs() times out.
Measure:
average response time,
maximum response time,
rows returned,
XML-RPC failures,
retry statistics.

Measure reconciliation health
Add metrics such as:

Total governed calls
CDR matched
Match %
Pending
Timeout
No CDR
Average lookup latency

This gives the NOC immediate visibility into whether reconciliation itself is healthy.

Capture SIP evidence for check calls
For the small number of remaining check calls (31–51 s), collect:

AMI Redirect timestamp,
AMI Hangup timestamp,
SIP BYE transmission,
SIP 200 OK to BYE,
Vendor Call-ID.

That will let you distinguish:

delayed BYE,
ignored BYE,
retransmissions,
or CDR mismatches.
What I would avoid concluding

I would avoid saying:

"The billing leak is completely fixed."

Instead I'd say:

The severe vendor-duration overruns observed on June 30 have not reappeared after the dialplan and recovery work. Governance timing is operating correctly. However, only a small percentage of governed calls currently have matched vendor CDRs, so end-to-end billing validation remains incomplete until reconciliation coverage is improved.

That statement is supported by the evidence you've gathered and accurately reflects the remaining uncertainty.

I think your next engineering milestone should be to raise CDR reconciliation coverage from ~6% to at least 95%. Once you can verify nearly every governed call against a vendor CDR, you'll have a much stronger basis for confirming that vendor billing consistently aligns with your governance rules.
