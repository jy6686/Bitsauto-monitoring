I would avoid making every module implement hierarchy independently.

I would make one small refinement:

Portal
      ↓
Role
      ↓
Hierarchy Scope
      ↓
Permission Check
      ↓
Data Filter
      ↓
Module

This separates what actions the user may perform (permissions such as "send rates" or "edit products") from which records they may access (hierarchy scope).

For example:

An AE may have permission to send rates, but only for their own customers.
A Team Lead may have the same permission for their team's customers.
A Manager may approve rate changes for their organization's customers.

The permission is identical; the visible dataset differs.

My recommendation

I would not treat this as a Commercial Portal sprint. I would define it as a Platform Hierarchical Data Access Framework because NOC, Finance, Commercial, and even Admin can all reuse it with different hierarchy definitions.

Then the Commercial Portal simply becomes the first consumer of that framework.

That keeps the architecture layered:

Portal → controls where users can go.
Permissions → controls what they can do.
Hierarchy Scope → controls which data they can access.
Modules → consume the filtered data.

That approach scales well and avoids every module reinventing access control.
