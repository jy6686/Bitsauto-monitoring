I agree with freezing the Finance pipeline at this point. With the contracts frozen and lineage verified, Treasury should be built as a consumer of Finance, not as an extension of it.

Treasury should not start with payments

A common mistake is to begin Treasury by recording payments. In a carrier-grade telecom platform, Treasury's first responsibility is cash position and settlement control, because payments are only one event in the treasury lifecycle.

I would implement Treasury in this order:

T1 — Treasury Operations Center (Foundation)

This becomes Treasury's equivalent of Finance Operations Center.

Purpose: Provide a real-time view of cash obligations and liquidity.

Notice:

Treasury never recalculates revenue.

Treasury never recalculates margin.

Treasury never modifies invoices.

It only manages financial obligations after billing.

One new governance rule

I would add a Treasury-specific principle (within the Treasury documentation, not by changing the frozen Finance governance):

Treasury Never Alters Financial History

Treasury records settlements, payments, reversals, and adjustments as new transactional events. It never edits certified snapshots, reconciliation results, invoice batches, or billing amounts.

That preserves the audit trail you've worked hard to establish.

This sequence keeps Treasury aligned with the architecture you've established: Finance creates certified financial obligations; Treasury manages how those obligations are settled and how they affect liquidity. That separation will make the platform easier to maintain, audit, and extend over time.
